**1,316 nouns** with their **article** and **plural form** — the two things you must know about every German noun. Collected from the whole A1–C1 course.

## Why this file exists

In German you cannot use a noun correctly without two pieces of information:

1. **the article** (`der`, `die`, `das`) — it determines the gender and the whole declension
2. **the plural form** — irregular and unpredictable (`der Tag → die Tage`, but `der Apfel → die Äpfel`)

A dictionary entry that gives only the meaning is useless. **This file gives all three: article, meaning, plural.**

## How to use this file

- **Search** with Ctrl+F for the noun you need.
- Learn each noun as a **triple**: `der Tag – die Tage`. Never learn the noun alone.
- The **Level** column shows where the noun appears (A1 → C1).
- Plural **`(no pl.)`** = normally singular only (mass, abstract, grammar terms). Plural **`—`** = normally plural only.
- **Read the accuracy note below before you trust a plural in an exam.**

## ⚠️ Accuracy note (please read)

- **606 plurals** are taken **directly from the course material** (the level vocabulary files and the topic data both carry a plural column). These are the reliable core.
- **545 plurals** are **derived** — from standard German plural rules — and are marked with a **`*`** in the Plural column. Treat these as a study aid, not as verified fact.
- **124 nouns** are marked `(no pl.)` (used only in the singular) and **41** are pluralia tantum (`—`, e.g. *die Eltern*).
- Most `*` entries follow hard rules (feminine `-ung/-heit/-keit/-schaft/-ion/-tät` → `-en`) and are safe. A minority are common irregulars.
- **If a `*` plural matters for an exam, verify it in dict.cc or DWDS first.** I would rather you check than memorise an error.
- Words marked **†** were added from the thematic topics in the Deutschbuch app (animals, food, the body, clothing, travel…). Their plurals come from the topic data, not the level files.

## Plural patterns: the reference

German plurals are not random. There are **five main patterns** plus foreign words.

| Pattern | Ending | Example | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **-e** | der Tag → die Tage | very common (masc./neut.) |
| 2 | **-er** | das Kind → die Kinder | common (neut.); often +umlaut |
| 3 | **-n / -en** | die Frau → die Frauen | almost all feminine nouns |
| 4 | **-s** | das Auto → die Autos | loanwords, abbreviations |
| 5 | **no ending** | der Lehrer → die Lehrer | masc./neut. in -er, -el, -en |
| 6 | **umlaut only** | der Apfel → die Äpfel | a/o/u → ä/ö/ü |

### The feminine rule (the most useful one)

**Almost every feminine noun adds `-n` or `-en` in the plural.**

| Singular | Plural |
|---|---|
| die Frau | die Frauen |
| die Blume | die Blumen |
| die Zeitung | die Zeitungen |
| die Möglichkeit | die Möglichkeiten |
| die Nation | die Nationen |

**Exception:** feminine nouns in `-in` double the n: `die Lehrerin → die Lehrerinnen`.

### Feminine suffixes that are ALWAYS -en in the plural

| Suffix | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| -ung | die Zeitung | die Zeitungen |
| -heit | die Freiheit | die Freiheiten |
| -keit | die Möglichkeit | die Möglichkeiten |
| -igkeit | die Nachhaltigkeit | die Nachhaltigkeiten |
| -schaft | die Freundschaft | die Freundschaften |
| -ion | die Nation | die Nationen |
| -tät | die Universität | die Universitäten |
| -ik | die Musik | die Musiken |
| -ei | die Bäckerei | die Bäckereien |

**All of these are feminine.** Learn the suffix and you know the gender *and* the plural.

### Masculine and neuter: the hard part

No reliable rule. Learn each one. Some tendencies:

| Ending | Tendency | Example |
|---|---|---|
| -er, -el, -en | often **no ending** | der Lehrer → die Lehrer |
| -chen, -lein | **no ending** (always neuter) | das Mädchen → die Mädchen |
| -ling, -ing | **-e** | der Prüfling → die Prüflinge |
| foreign -or, -ist, -ent, -ant | **-en** | der Student → die Studenten |
| neuter -ment, -at, -ent, -iv | **-e** | das Dokument → die Dokumente |

### The n-Deklination (weak masculine nouns)

A special group of **masculine** nouns adds **-en/-n in ALL cases**, not just the plural.

| Noun | Plural | Also in Akkusativ/Dativ |
|---|---|---|
| der Junge | die Jungen | den Jungen, dem Jungen |
| der Mensch | die Menschen | den Menschen, dem Menschen |
| der Student | die Studenten | den Studenten, dem Studenten |
| der Kollege | die Kollegen | den Kollegen, dem Kollegen |
| der Herr | die Herren | den Herrn, dem Herrn |

Recognise the pattern: **masculine + (often) a person → weak noun.**

## Study method

1. **Never write a noun without its article and plural.** This file is your model.
2. **Use Anki** with cards that show the singular and ask for article + plural.
3. **Learn the suffix rules first** (`-ung`, `-heit`, `-keit` are always feminine and always `-en`). That covers a large share of abstract nouns instantly.
4. **Colour-code by gender** in your notes: der = blue, die = red, das = green.
5. **Verify `*` entries** in dict.cc before relying on them in an exam.
