# Lektion 08: Adjektivdeklination (Adjective Endings)

**Level:** A2 | **Target time:** 30 minutes, repeat over 5 sessions | **Status:** not started

---

## Goal

Put the correct ending on an adjective that comes before a noun, in all three patterns.

## Why this matters

This is the most **feared** topic in German grammar, but it is mostly memorisation, not logic. Once you see that it is a table with three patterns, it becomes mechanical.

The reason adjectives change is simple: the article sometimes does not show the gender clearly, so the **adjective has to carry the information** instead. German never leaves the noun's gender ambiguous.

**Relax.** Even native speakers do not think about this consciously. Learn the three tables, drill them, and they become automatic. Getting it slightly wrong is common and usually still understood.

---

## Material

### The three patterns

There are three situations, depending on what comes before the adjective:

| Pattern | What comes before | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Weak** | der / die / das, dieser, jener | **der** gut**e** Wein |
| **Mixed** | ein / kein / mein (possessives) | **ein** gut**er** Wein |
| **Strong** | nothing (no article) | gut**er** Wein |

### Why the ending changes

The gender must be visible somewhere. If the article already shows it (`der`), the adjective can be lazy and just add `-e`. If there is no article, the adjective must show the full gender, so it looks like `der`/`die`/`das` itself: `gut**er**`, `gut**e**`, `gut**es**`.

### Pattern 1: Weak (after der / die / das)

The article does the work, so the adjective is mostly `-e` or `-en`.

| | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter | Plural |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nominativ | der gut**e** | die gut**e** | das gut**e** | die gut**en** |
| Akkusativ | den gut**en** | die gut**e** | das gut**e** | die gut**en** |
| Dativ | dem gut**en** | der gut**en** | dem gut**en** | den gut**en** |
| Genitiv | des gut**en** | der gut**en** | des gut**en** | der gut**en** |

**Rule of thumb for weak:** if the article shows the case clearly, the adjective is `-e`. Otherwise `-en`. The only `-e` forms are the five where der/die/das is unambiguous: nominative all genders, and accusative feminine/neuter. Everything else is `-en`.

### Pattern 2: Mixed (after ein / kein / mein)

Here the article sometimes fails to show gender (ein has no masculine/neuter distinction), so the adjective must help.

| | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter | Plural (kein/mein) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nominativ | ein gut**er** | eine gut**e** | ein gut**es** | keine gut**en** |
| Akkusativ | einen gut**en** | eine gut**e** | ein gut**es** | keine gut**en** |
| Dativ | einem gut**en** | einer gut**en** | einem gut**en** | keinen gut**en** |

**Key point:** in the mixed pattern, the nominative masculine and neuter take the strong ending (`-er`, `-es`) because `ein` does not show the gender. Everywhere else, `-en`.

### Pattern 3: Strong (no article)

The adjective must carry the whole gender signal, so it looks like `der`/`die`/`das`.

| | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter | Plural |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nominativ | gut**er** Wein | gut**e** Milch | gut**es** Bier | gut**e** Weine |
| Akkusativ | gut**en** Wein | gut**e** Milch | gut**es** Bier | gut**e** Weine |
| Dativ | gut**em** Wein | gut**er** Milch | gut**em** Bier | gut**en** Weinen |
| Genitiv | gut**en** Weins | gut**er** Milch | gut**en** Biers | gut**er** Weine |

The endings mirror the definite article: `-er` (der), `-e` (die), `-es` (das), `-em` (dem), `-en` (den).

### The shortcut: which pattern am I in?

Ask: what word is in front of the adjective?

| Word before | Pattern |
|---|---|
| der, die, das, dieser, jeder, welcher | Weak |
| ein, eine, kein, mein, dein, sein, ihr, unser, euer, Ihr | Mixed |
| nothing | Strong |

### Adjective endings after the prepositions

If a preposition comes before, use the case the preposition requires. `mit dem gut**en** Wein` (dative, weak).

### Comparison with English

English has one adjective form: "the good wine", "a good wine", "good wine". German changes the ending in every case. This is why it feels heavy at first. But it is finite: three tables, learned once.

### Practical advice

1. Learn the **weak** table first. It is the most common (after der/die/das).
2. Then the **mixed** table (after ein/mein).
3. The **strong** table (no article) is least common in speech but appears in ads and headlines.
4. Drill with real nouns you already know, with their gender.

## Pronunciation note

The endings are unstressed: `gute` sounds like "GOO-te", `guten` like "GOO-ten". Do not over-pronounce them; they are light.

## Common mistakes

- Wrong: `ein gute Mann`. Right: `ein guter Mann`. Mixed nominative masculine takes -er.
- Wrong: `ein neu Auto`. Right: `ein neues Auto`. Mixed nominative neuter takes -es.
- Wrong: using -e everywhere after der. Right: most weak forms are -en, only five are -e.
- Wrong: forgetting that plural weak is always -en. Right: `die guten Männer`, `die guten Frauen`, `die guten Kinder`.
- Wrong: panicking about the tables. Right: learn them as three fixed patterns and drill.
