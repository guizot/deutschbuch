# Lektion 01: Der Dativ (The Dative Case)

**Level:** A2 | **Target time:** 30 minutes, repeat over 4 sessions | **Status:** not started

---

## Goal

Understand the dative case, change articles and pronouns correctly, and learn the prepositions and verbs that always take the dative.

## Why this matters

This is the **most important lesson in A2**, exactly as gender was in A1. The dative appears constantly: after certain verbs (helfen, gefallen), after certain prepositions (mit, nach, bei), and as the indirect object. If A1 taught you that articles change, A2 teaches you there is a **second** set of changes.

Good news: the dative has more changes than the accusative, but they follow a pattern you can memorise in one table.

## Material

### The accusative vs the dative

| Case | Function | Question | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nominativ | Subject | Wer? Was? | **Der Mann** liest. |
| Akkusativ | Direct object | Wen? Was? | Ich sehe **den Mann**. |
| Dativ | Indirect object | Wem? | Ich helfe **dem Mann**. |

`Ich gebe dem Mann das Buch.` — I give the man the book.
`dem Mann` = dative (the receiver), `das Buch` = accusative (the thing given).

### Definite articles in the dative

| | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter | Plural |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nominative | der | die | das | die |
| Akkusativ | den | die | das | die |
| **Dativ** | **dem** | **der** | **dem** | **den** + noun gets **-n** |

**Two things to notice:**
1. Feminine changes to `der` in the dative. So `der` appears twice, but means different things.
2. Plural adds **-n** to the noun: `die Kinder` → `den Kindern`, `die Freunde` → `den Freunden`.

### Indefinite, negative, and possessive articles

| | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter | Plural |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Indefinite | einem | einer | einem | - |
| Negative | keinem | keiner | keinem | keinen + n |
| Possessive (mein) | meinem | meiner | meinem | meinen + n |

### The plural -n rule

In the dative plural, the noun adds **-n**, unless it already ends in -n or -s.

| Nominative plural | Dative plural |
|---|---|
| die Kinder | den Kindern |
| die Freunde | den Freunden |
| die Häuser | den Häusern |
| die Autos | den Autos (already ends in -s) |
| die Frauen | den Frauen (already ends in -n) |

### Dative pronouns

| Nominative | Accusative | Dative |
|---|---|---|
| ich | mich | **mir** |
| du | dich | **dir** |
| er | ihn | **ihm** |
| sie | sie | **ihr** |
| es | es | **ihm** |
| wir | uns | **uns** |
| ihr | euch | **euch** |
| sie / Sie | sie / Sie | **ihnen / Ihnen** |

Examples: `Kannst du **mir** helfen?` (Can you help me?) `Wie geht es **dir**?` (How are you?)

### Dative prepositions (always dative)

Memorise these eight. They **always** take the dative, no exceptions.

| Preposition | English | Example |
|---|---|---|
| aus | from, out of | aus **der** Schweiz |
| bei | at, with (a person) | bei **meinem** Bruder |
| mit | with | mit **dem** Bus |
| nach | after, to (cities/countries) | nach **der** Arbeit |
| seit | since, for (time) | seit **einem** Jahr |
| von | from, of | von **der** Arbeit |
| zu | to (a place/person) | zu **dem** Arzt (zum Arzt) |
| gegenüber | opposite | gegenüber **dem** Bahnhof |

**Memory trick:** sing them as **"aus, bei, mit, nach, seit, von, zu"** to a rhythm. These are among the most frequent words in German.

Contractions: `zu dem` → **zum**, `zu der` → **zur**, `bei dem` → **beim**, `von dem` → **vom**.

### Verbs that take the dative

Some verbs take a dative object instead of an accusative. There is no logic; learn them as a set.

| Verb | English | Example |
|---|---|---|
| helfen | to help | Ich helfe **dir**. |
| gefallen | to please, to like | Das Buch gefällt **mir**. |
| gehören | to belong to | Das gehört **mir**. |
| passen | to fit, suit | Die Hose passt **mir**. |
| danken | to thank | Ich danke **dir**. |
| antworten | to answer | Er antwortet **mir**. |
| glauben | to believe | Ich glaube **dir**. |
| folgen | to follow | Folge **mir**! |
| schmecken | to taste | Die Suppe schmeckt **mir**. |
| wehtun | to hurt | Mein Kopf tut **mir** weh. |
| zuhören | to listen to | Hör **mir** zu! |
| gratulieren | to congratulate | Ich gratuliere **dir**. |

**Note the pattern:** in all these, the person is the dative object. `Ich helfe dir` literally = "I give help to you".

**Important contrast:** `gefallen` works backwards from English.
`Das Buch gefällt mir.` = "The book is pleasing to me" = **I like the book.**
The book is the subject, the person is the dative object.

### Free dative (bonus)

Sometimes the dative is added for interest: `Ich wasche **mir** die Hände.` (I wash my hands.) This is common and natural, but you do not need to produce it actively at A2.

**1. Dative articles.** Fill in the correct definite article.

d) Er kommt ______ Deutschland.
e) ______ der Arbeit gehe ich nach Hause.
f) Die Bank ist ______ dem Supermarkt.

**5. Dative verb or accusative verb?**

## Pronunciation note

`dem` is "dame", `den` is "dane", `der` is "dair". The difference between accusative `den` and dative `dem` is one letter and a lot of meaning. Practise: `Ich sehe den Mann` vs `Ich helfe dem Mann`.

## Common mistakes

- Wrong: `Ich helfe den Mann`. Right: `Ich helfe dem Mann`. helfen takes the dative.
- Wrong: `Ich fahre mit der Bus`. Right: `mit dem Bus`. mit always takes the dative.
- Wrong: `Das gefällt ich`. Right: `Das gefällt mir`. The person is the dative object.
- Wrong: forgetting the -n in dative plural: `mit die Kinder`. Right: `mit den Kindern`.
- Wrong: `zu dem` in fast speech. Right: `zum` is the contraction you will hear and should use.
