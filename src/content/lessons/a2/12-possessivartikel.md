# Lektion 12: Possessivartikel (Possessive Articles)

**Level:** A2 | **Target time:** 30 minutes, repeat over 3 sessions | **Status:** not started

---

## Goal

Use "my, your, his, her, our, their" correctly in all four cases, and match them to the gender of the thing owned.

## Why this matters

Possessives are everywhere in daily speech: my family, your job, his car, our house. The tricky part is that German possessives change ending depending on the **case** and the **gender of the owned noun**, not the owner.

---

## Material

### The possessive stems

Each person has a possessive stem. The ending is added to it.

| Person | Possessive stem | English |
|---|---|---|
| ich | mein- | my |
| du | dein- | your (informal) |
| er / es | sein- | his, its |
| sie | ihr- | her |
| wir | unser- | our |
| ihr | euer- | your (plural) |
| sie | ihr- | their |
| Sie | Ihr- | your (formal) |

### The base forms (nominative)

| Person | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter | Plural |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ich | mein | meine | mein | meine |
| du | dein | deine | dein | deine |
| er | sein | seine | sein | seine |
| sie | ihr | ihre | ihr | ihre |
| wir | unser | unsere | unser | unsere |
| ihr | euer | eure | euer | eure |
| sie | ihr | ihre | ihr | ihre |
| Sie | Ihr | Ihre | Ihr | Ihre |

**Note:** `euer` drops the -e- when an ending is added: `euer` → `eure`, `eurem`.

### The endings follow the ein/kein pattern

Possessives behave exactly like `ein` and `kein`. In the nominative masculine and neuter, they take **no ending**. Everywhere else, they take the ending that shows the case and gender.

| | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter | Plural |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nominativ | mein | meine | mein | meine |
| Akkusativ | mein**en** | meine | mein | meine |
| Dativ | mein**em** | mein**er** | mein**em** | mein**en** |
| Genitiv | mein**es** | mein**er** | mein**es** | mein**er** |

### The key insight: the possessive agrees with the OWNED thing

The possessive ending depends on the **gender of the noun it is attached to**, not the owner.

`mein Vater` (my father, masculine)
`meine Mutter` (my mother, feminine)
`mein Kind` (my child, neuter)
`meine Eltern` (my parents, plural)

And it changes for the owner too:
`sein Vater` (his father)
`ihre Mutter` (her mother)
`unser Kind` (our child)

### In the accusative

| | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter | Plural |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ich | meinen | meine | mein | meine |
| du | deinen | deine | dein | deine |
| er | seinen | seine | sein | seine |
| sie | ihren | ihre | ihr | ihre |

`Ich sehe **meinen** Vater.` (masculine accusative: meinen)
`Ich sehe **meine** Mutter.` (feminine accusative: meine)
`Ich sehe **mein** Kind.` (neuter accusative: mein)

### In the dative

| | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter | Plural |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ich | meinem | meiner | meinem | meinen |
| du | deinem | deiner | deinem | deinen |
| er | seinem | seiner | seinem | seinen |
| sie | ihrem | ihrer | ihrem | ihren |

`Ich helfe **meinem** Vater.` (dative masculine)
`Ich helfe **meiner** Mutter.` (dative feminine)
`Ich helfe **meinen** Eltern.` (dative plural, +n on noun if needed)

### Possessive in a sentence: examples

| German | English |
|---|---|
| Mein Name ist Rinaldi. | My name is Rinaldi. |
| Meine Familie wohnt in Jakarta. | My family lives in Jakarta. |
| Das ist mein Bruder. | That is my brother. |
| Ich liebe meine Arbeit. | I love my work. |
| Wo ist dein Handy? | Where is your phone? |
| Wie ist deine Adresse? | What is your address? |
| Sein Auto ist neu. | His car is new. |
| Ihre Wohnung ist groß. | Her apartment is big. |
| Unser Haus ist klein. | Our house is small. |
| Wie ist eure Schule? | How is your (plural) school? |
| Ihr Hund ist süß. | Their dog is cute. |
| Ist das Ihr Koffer? | Is that your (formal) suitcase? |

### sein vs ihr: watch the context

`sein` = his (or its), `ihr` = her or their. The context tells you.

`Sein Vater ist Arzt.` (His father is a doctor.)
`Ihr Vater ist Arzt.` (Her father / their father is a doctor.)

### Capitalised Ihr = formal you

`Ihr` with a capital I is the formal "your".
`Wie ist **Ihr** Name?` (What is your name? — formal)
`Ist das **Ihre** Tasche?` (Is that your bag? — formal)

### Possessives as pronouns

You can also use possessives alone (without a noun), and then they take different endings, mostly like `der/die/das`:

`Das ist mein Auto.` → `Das ist **meins**.` (That is mine.)
`Das ist deine Tasche.` → `Das ist **deine**.` (That is yours.)

At A2, recognising these is enough; producing them actively is a B1 skill.

## Pronunciation note

`meine` is "MY-ne". `meinem` is "MY-nem". `euer` is "OY-er", and `eure` is "OY-re". The endings are light.

## Common mistakes

- Wrong: `Mein Mutter`. Right: `Meine Mutter`. Mutter is feminine.
- Wrong: `Ich sehe mein Vater`. Right: `meinen Vater`. Accusative masculine takes -en.
- Wrong: `Ich helfe mein Bruder`. Right: `meinem Bruder`. Dative masculine takes -em.
- Wrong: `dein Tasche`. Right: `deine Tasche`. Feminine takes -e.
- Wrong: matching the possessive to the owner instead of the owned thing. Right: the ending follows the **owned noun's** gender and case.
