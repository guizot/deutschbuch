# Lektion 06: Der Akkusativ (The Accusative Case)

**Level:** A1 | **Target time:** 30 minutes, repeat over 4 sessions | **Status:** not started

---

## Goal

Understand what a case is, recognise the accusative (the direct object), and change articles correctly in the accusative.

## Why this matters

This is the **second most important lesson in A1**, after gender. German marks the role of a noun in the sentence by changing its article. The accusative is the first case you meet, and it is the gateway to Dativ, Genitiv, and every adjective ending later.

Good news: the accusative is the **easiest** case. Only the masculine article changes. Feminine, neuter, and plural look exactly the same as the nominative.

---

## Material

### What is a case?

A case shows the **function** of a noun in the sentence: is it the doer, the receiver, or something else?

| Case | Function | Question | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nominativ | Subject (the doer) | Wer? Was? | **Der Mann** kauft ein Auto. |
| Akkusativ | Direct object (the receiver) | Wen? Was? | Ich sehe **den Mann**. |
| Dativ | Indirect object | Wem? | Ich gebe **dem Mann** das Buch. |
| Genitiv | Possession | Wessen? | Das Auto **des Mannes**. |

At A1 you need Nominativ and Akkusativ. Dativ comes at A2.

### The accusative change

**Only masculine changes.** `der` becomes `den`. Everything else stays.

| Gender | Nominative | Accusative | Change? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Masculine | der | **den** | Yes |
| Feminine | die | die | No |
| Neuter | das | das | No |
| Plural | die | die | No |

### Articles in the accusative

| | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter | Plural |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Definite | den | die | das | die |
| Indefinite | einen | eine | ein | - (no article) |
| Negative | keinen | keine | kein | keine |
| Possessive | meinen | meine | mein | meine |

### Personal pronouns in the accusative

| Nominative | Accusative |
|---|---|
| ich | mich |
| du | dich |
| er | ihn |
| sie | sie |
| es | es |
| wir | uns |
| ihr | euch |
| sie / Sie | sie / Sie |

Examples: `Ich sehe ihn.` (I see him.) `Kennst du mich?` (Do you know me?)

### When to use the accusative

The accusative is used for the **direct object**: the thing directly affected by the verb.

`Ich kaufe **den Tisch**.` — I buy the table. (The table is what I buy.)
`Er liest **das Buch**.` — He reads the book.
`Wir sehen **die Frau**.` — We see the woman.

**Verbs that take the accusative** (most verbs do):

| Verb | English | Example |
|---|---|---|
| haben | to have | Ich habe **einen** Hund. |
| kaufen | to buy | Ich kaufe **den** Tisch. |
| sehen | to see | Ich sehe **den** Mann. |
| lesen | to read | Er liest **das** Buch. |
| essen | to eat | Sie isst **einen** Apfel. |
| trinken | to drink | Ich trinke **den** Kaffee. |
| suchen | to search | Ich suche **meinen** Schlüssel. |
| finden | to find | Ich finde **den** Weg. |
| brauchen | to need | Wir brauchen **einen** Tisch. |
| nehmen | to take | Ich nehme **den** Bus. |
| besuchen | to visit | Ich besuche **meinen** Freund. |
| kennen | to know (person) | Kennst du **den** Mann? |
| mögen | to like | Ich mag **den** Film. |

### Accusative prepositions

Some prepositions **always** take the accusative. Memorise this set:

| Preposition | English | Example |
|---|---|---|
| durch | through | durch **den** Park |
| für | for | für **meinen** Bruder |
| gegen | against | gegen **den** Wind |
| ohne | without | ohne **einen** Plan |
| um | around, at | um **den** Baum, um **die** Ecke |
| bis | until | bis **den** Montag (rare) |
| entlang | along | **den** Fluss entlang |

A useful memory trick: **durch, für, gegen, ohne, um** spell "DufGeOu". Some learners use the word **"FUDGO"** or sing it. Any device is fine, as long as it becomes automatic.

### Time expressions in the accusative

Some time expressions use the accusative without a preposition:

`Ich arbeite **jeden Tag**.` (I work every day.)
`**Nächste Woche** fahre ich nach Berlin.` (Next week I go to Berlin.)

d) Wir besuchen die Kinder. → Wir besuchen ______.

## Pronunciation note

`den` sounds like "dane" (short). `dem` (Dativ, A2) is "dame". Practise the difference: `den Mann` vs `dem Mann`.

## Common mistakes

- Wrong: changing all articles in the accusative. Right: only masculine changes: `der` → `den`, `ein` → `einen`.
- Wrong: `Ich sehe der Mann`. Right: `Ich sehe den Mann`. Direct object = accusative.
- Wrong: forgetting the accusative after `für`, `ohne`, `durch`, `gegen`, `um`. Right: those always take the accusative.
- Wrong: `Ich habe kein Hund`. Right: `Ich habe keinen Hund` (masculine accusative).
