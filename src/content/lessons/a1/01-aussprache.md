# Lektion 01: Aussprache (Pronunciation)

**Level:** A1 | **Target time:** 30 minutes, repeat over 3 sessions | **Status:** not started

---

## Goal

Read any German word out loud and be understood. German spelling is close to pronunciation, so this pays off immediately and permanently.

## Why this matters

German is far more phonetic than English. Once you know the rules, you can pronounce a word you have never seen. The reverse is also true: habits you build now will stick, so build good ones. Bad pronunciation is also the fastest way to not be understood in a shop or an exam.

## Material

### The alphabet

German uses the same 26 letters, plus **ä ö ü** (umlauts) and **ß** (Eszett, "sharp s").

| Letter | Name | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| A a | ah | "ah" in father |
| B b | beh | b |
| C c | tseh | mostly in "ch", see below |
| D d | deh | d |
| E e | eh | "e" in bed |
| F f | ef | f |
| G g | geh | hard g, "go" |
| H h | hah | h, but silent after a vowel (see below) |
| I i | ih | "i" in sit |
| J j | yot | **y** sound, like "yes" |
| K k | kah | k |
| L l | el | l |
| M m | em | m |
| N n | en | n |
| O o | oh | "o" in more |
| P p | peh | p |
| Q q | kuh | always "qu" = kv |
| R r | er | guttural, see below |
| S s | es | z before a vowel, s otherwise |
| T t | teh | t |
| U u | uh | "oo" in moon |
| V v | fau | **f** sound, like "father" |
| W w | veh | **v** sound, like "van" |
| X x | iks | ks |
| Y y | ypsilon | ü or y |
| Z z | tsett | **ts**, like "cats" |
| Ä ä | äh | "e" in bed, longer |
| Ö ö | öh | like "u" in fur, rounded lips |
| Ü ü | üh | like "u" in French "tu" |
| ß | eszett | ss |

**Key traps:** J = "y" (not "j"), V = "f" (not "v"), W = "v" (not "w"), Z = "ts" (not "z"). German "ei" = "eye", German "ie" = "ee".

### Special combinations

| Spelling | Sound | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **ei** | "eye" | mein, Eis, arbeiten |
| **ie** | "ee" | Liebe, Bier, vier |
| **eu / äu** | "oy" | neu, heute, Häuser |
| **au** | "ow" | Haus, blau, Frau |
| **sch** | "sh" | Schule, Fisch |
| **sp** (start of word) | "shp" | sprechen, Sport |
| **st** (start of word) | "sht" | Stadt, stehen |
| **ch** (after a, o, u, au) | harsh, back of throat | ach, Buch, auch |
| **ch** (after e, i, ä, ö, ü) | soft, like "hue" | ich, Milch, Bücher |
| **-ig** (end of word) | "ich" sound | zwanzig, wichtig |
| **-er** (end of word) | "ah", soft | Vater, Wasser, Kinder |
| **v** | "f" | Vater, viel |
| **z** | "ts" | Zeit, zehn |
| **qu** | "kv" | Quelle, bequem |
| **-tion** | "tsee-ohn" | Information, Nation |
| **-ss / ß** | "s" | Straße, essen |

### The letter R

Two ways, both fine at A1:
- **After a vowel** (Vater, der, wir): the r softens to an "ah" sound. "Vater" is roughly "FAH-tah".
- **At the start of a word** (rot, Rad): a guttural sound from the back of the throat, similar to French.

Do not roll it like Spanish. Do not use the English r.

### The letter H

- At the start of a word or after a consonant: normal h. `Haus`, `haben`
- **After a vowel**: silent, it just lengthens the vowel. `fahren` = "FAH-ren", `Zahl` = "tsahl"

### Umlauts

Umlauts change the sound. They are not optional decoration.

| Without | With | Difference |
|---|---|---|
| a | ä | father -> bed |
| o | ö | more -> fur, rounded |
| u | ü | moon -> French "tu", rounded |

Wrong umlaut can change the word: `schon` (already) vs `schön` (beautiful), `Bruder` (brother) vs `Brüder` (brothers).

### Stress

Usually on the **first syllable**: `ARbeit`, `MUtter`, `LEHrer`.
Exceptions: words with `be-`, `ge-`, `er-`, `ver-`, `zer-` stress the second syllable: `beKOMMen`, `verSTEHen`, `geFALLen`.

## Exercises

**1. Read aloud, then check the hint.** What sound does each make?

a) Zeit
b) Jahr
c) Wasser

**2. Long or short vowel?** Say each aloud. Which have a long vowel?

a) Stadt
b) Staat
c) Zahl
d) Sonne

**3. ei or ie?** Fill in the correct sound ("eye" or "ee").

a) mein
b) Liebe
c) vier
d) Eis
e) Spiegel
f) arbeiten

**4. Silent h or sounded h?**

a) Haus
b) fahren
c) haben
d) Zahl

**5. Stress.** Mark the stressed syllable.

a) Arbeit
b) verstehen
c) Mutter
d) bekommen
e) Lehrer

**6. Umlaut trap.** What is the difference in meaning?

a) schon / schön
b) Bruder / Brüder
c) Mutter / Mütter

### Answer key

**1.**
a) Zeit = "tsite" (ts sound)
b) Jahr = "yahr" (y sound)
c) Wasser = "VAH-sah" (v sound, -er softens)
d) Vogel = "FOH-gel" (v = f sound)
e) Schule = "SHOO-le" (sch = sh)
f) ich = soft "kh" from the front, like "ish" with a scrape
g) auch = harsh "kh" from the back
h) Bier = "beer" (ie = ee)

**2.**
a) Stadt = short
b) Staat = long (double vowel)
c) Zahl = long (silent h lengthens)
d) Sonne = short (double consonant shortens)

**3.**
a) mein = eye
b) Liebe = ee
c) vier = ee
d) Eis = eye
e) Spiegel = ee
f) arbeiten = eye

**4.**
a) Haus = sounded
b) fahren = silent, lengthens the a
c) haben = sounded
d) Zahl = silent, lengthens the a

**5.**
a) **AR**beit
b) ver**STE**hen
c) **MU**tter
d) be**KOM**men
e) **LEH**rer

**6.**
a) schon = already, schön = beautiful
b) Bruder = brother, Brüder = brothers
c) Mutter = mother, Mütter = mothers

## Pronunciation note

The two `ch` sounds are the ones that most mark an English accent. Practice:
- Soft (after e, i): **ich**, **Milch**, **Bücher**
- Hard (after a, o, u): **ach**, **Buch**, **auch**

Say "ich" with your mouth spread, then "ach" with your mouth open. That difference is worth drilling.

## Common mistakes

- Wrong: "Jahr" as "jar". Right: "yahr". German J is a y sound.
- Wrong: "Vater" as "vater" with a v. Right: "fah-tah". German V is an f sound.
- Wrong: "Wein" as "wine" with an English w. Right: German W uses the lips and teeth like a v.
- Wrong: rolling the R like Spanish. Right: soft after a vowel, guttural at the start.
- Wrong: ignoring umlauts. Right: they change the word.
