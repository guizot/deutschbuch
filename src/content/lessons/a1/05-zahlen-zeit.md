# Lektion 05: Zahlen, Uhrzeit und Datum (Numbers, Time and Dates)

**Level:** A1 | **Target time:** 30 minutes, repeat over 3 sessions | **Status:** not started

---

## Goal

Count, tell the time, say dates, and handle prices. This is pure survival German and it appears in every exam and every shop.

## Why this matters

Numbers and time are the vocabulary you cannot avoid. They also have a quirk that trips up everyone: German says numbers **backwards** (one-and-twenty), and time uses `halb` differently from English.

### The backwards rule (21-99)

German says the **ones digit first**, then **und**, then the tens.

| Number | German | Literal |
|---|---|---|
| 21 | einundzwanzig | one-and-twenty |
| 32 | zweiunddreißig | two-and-thirty |
| 45 | fünfundvierzig | five-and-forty |
| 67 | siebenundsechzig | seven-and-sixty |
| 99 | neunundneunzig | nine-and-ninety |

**This is written as one word.** `einundzwanzig`, not `ein und zwanzig`.

### Prices

German uses a comma for decimals.

| Written | Said |
|---|---|
| 3,50 € | drei Euro fünfzig |
| 12,99 € | zwölf Euro neunundneunzig |
| 1.200 € | tausendzweihundert Euro (or zwölfhundert) |

The dot is the thousands separator, the comma is the decimal. Opposite of English.

### Telling time: the official way

24-hour clock. Used for schedules, appointments, official times.

| Time | German |
|---|---|
| 8:00 | acht Uhr |
| 9:15 | neun Uhr fünfzehn |
| 14:30 | vierzehn Uhr dreißig |
| 18:45 | achtzehn Uhr fünfundvierzig |

### Telling time: the everyday way

12-hour clock with special words. Common in speech.

| Time | German | Literal |
|---|---|---|
| 8:00 | acht Uhr | |
| 8:15 | Viertel nach acht | quarter past eight |
| 8:30 | **halb neun** | half **nine** = half TO nine, i.e. 8:30 |
| 8:45 | Viertel vor neun | quarter to nine |
| 8:05 | fünf nach acht | five past eight |
| 8:55 | fünf vor neun | five to nine |

**Critical trap:** `halb neun` means **8:30**, not 9:30. German `halb` means "halfway to" the next hour. This is the single most common time mistake for English speakers.

### Asking about time and date

| German | English |
|---|---|
| Wie viel Uhr ist es? | What time is it? |
| Wie spät ist es? | What time is it? (lit. how late) |
| Es ist acht Uhr. | It is eight o'clock. |
| Wann? | When? |
| Um wie viel Uhr? | At what time? |
| Welcher Tag ist heute? | What day is it today? |
| Heute ist Montag. | Today is Monday. |
| Der wievielte ist heute? | What is the date today? |

### Time prepositions

| Preposition | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| um | clock time | um acht Uhr (at 8) |
| am | days and dates | am Montag, am 1. Mai |
| im | months and seasons | im Januar, im Sommer |
| von ... bis | from ... to | von 9 bis 17 Uhr |

## Pronunciation note

`zwei` is "tsvy". `dreißig` is "DRY-sikh". `zwanzig` is "TSVAHN-tsikh" (the -ig becomes an ich sound). `sechzig` is "ZEKH-tsikh".

## Common mistakes

- Wrong: `halb neun` = 9:30. Right: 8:30. `halb` means halfway **to** the next hour.
- Wrong: writing `ein und zwanzig` as three words. Right: `einundzwanzig`, one word.
- Wrong: writing 1,200 for one thousand two hundred. Right: `1.200` in German. Comma and dot are swapped.
- Wrong: `sechszehn` and `siebenzehn`. Right: `sechzehn` and `siebzehn`.
- Wrong: `dreizig`. Right: `dreißig`.
