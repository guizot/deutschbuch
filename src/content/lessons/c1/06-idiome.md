# Lektion 06: Idiome und feste Wendungen

**Level:** C1 | **Target time:** 30 minutes, repeat over 4 sessions | **Status:** not started

---

## Goal

Understand and use the common German idioms and fixed expressions that make speech sound natural.

## Why this matters

Idioms are the difference between grammatically correct German and natural German. Native speakers use them constantly, and they appear in films, podcasts, and literature. Understanding them is essential for listening comprehension; using a few correctly is the fastest way to sound fluent at C1.

---

## Material

### What an idiom is

A fixed phrase whose meaning is not the sum of its parts.

`Daumen drücken` (literally "press thumbs") = to keep one's fingers crossed.
`Tomaten auf den Augen haben` (literally "to have tomatoes on the eyes") = to be unobservant.

You cannot translate these word-for-word; learn them as units.

### Learning strategy

1. **Learn idioms in context**, not in lists.
2. **Note the register**: some are colloquial (blau machen), some formal (im Rahmen).
3. **Use one or two** per conversation; too many sounds artificial.
4. **Read them in film subtitles** and podcasts.
5. **Group by theme** (body parts, animals, colours) for memory.

### The register warning

Some idioms are **colloquial** and inappropriate in formal writing.

| Colloquial | Formal alternative |
|---|---|
| blau machen | der Arbeit fernbleiben |
| einen Vogel haben | nicht zurechnungsfähig sein |
| Tomaten auf den Augen haben | unaufmerksam sein |

Use formal expressions in formal text.

### Practical C1 rules

1. **Understand idioms** passively first; production comes later.
2. **Note the register** of each.
3. **Use them sparingly** in speech.
4. **Avoid colloquial idioms** in formal writing.
5. **Learn the formal fixed expressions** for academic writing.

## Pronunciation note

Idioms are spoken as units, often quickly. `Daumen drücken` is "DOW-men DRÜ-ken". `unter vier Augen` is "UN-ter feer OW-gen". Practise the rhythm as a chunk.

## Common mistakes

- Wrong: translating idioms word-for-word.
- Wrong: using colloquial idioms in formal writing.
- Wrong: overusing idioms (sounds artificial).
- Wrong: getting the verb form wrong inside the idiom.
- Wrong: mixing the idiom's fixed words.
