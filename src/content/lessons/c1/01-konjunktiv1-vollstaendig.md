# Lektion 01: Der Konjunktiv I — Das vollständige System

**Level:** C1 | **Target time:** 30 minutes, repeat over 4 sessions | **Status:** not started

---

## Goal

Master the Konjunktiv I in all tenses and persons, report speech accurately, and use it as the register marker of journalism and academic German.

## Why this matters

At B2 you learned the Konjunktiv I for simple reported speech. At C1 you command the full system: all tenses, the substitution rules, and the subtle distinction between neutral reporting (K-I) and doubtful reporting (K-II). This is the single most distinctive feature of German journalistic and academic prose, and reading Die Zeit or a paper without it is impossible.

## Material

### The full Konjunktiv I system

| Tense | Formation | Example (sagen) |
|---|---|---|
| Präsens | stem + K-I ending | er sage |
| Perfekt | K-I of haben/sein + P2 | er habe gesagt |
| Futur I | K-I of werden + Inf. | er werde sagen |
| Futur II | K-I of werden + P2 + haben/sein | er werde gesagt haben |

### The K-I endings (present)

| Person | machen | sein | haben | werden | können |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ich | mache | sei | habe | werde | könne |
| du | machest | sei(e)st | habest | werdest | könnest |
| er/sie/es | mache | sei | habe | werde | könne |
| wir | machen | seien | haben | werden | können |
| ihr | machet | seiet | habet | werdet | könnet |
| sie/Sie | machen | seien | haben | werden | können |

**Key observation:** the **first and third person singular** take -e (mache, habe, werde, könne). This is where the K-I differs most from the indicative.

### The substitution rule (Ersatzregel)

When the K-I form is **identical** to the indicative, German replaces it with the **Konjunktiv II**.

| Verb / person | K-I | Indicative | Use instead |
|---|---|---|---|
| gehen (ich) | gehe | gehe | ginge (K-II) |
| gehen (wir) | gehen | gehen | gingen (K-II) |
| haben (wir) | haben | haben | hätten (K-II) |
| werden (sie) | werden | werden | würden (K-II) |
| sein (wir) | seien | sind | seien (differs, keep) |
| können (wir) | können | können | könnten (K-II) |

`Sie sagten, sie **gingen** nach Berlin.` (not `gehen` — identical to indicative)
`Sie sagten, sie **hätten** Zeit.` (not `haben`)

**The rule applies per form**, not per verb. `ich gehe` → `ginge`, but `er gehe` is fine (differs from `geht`).

### The two-level system in journalism

German journalists use K-I for the reported claim, and K-II when the claim is doubtful or when K-I is ambiguous.

`Der Minister sagte, die Lage **sei** unter Kontrolle.` (neutral report — K-I)
`Der Minister sagte, die Lage **wäre** unter Kontrolle.` (doubtful — K-II)

**`sei` = neutral. `wäre` = the journalist distances himself.**

This distinction is a C1 reading skill: it tells you the article's stance.

### Reported speech without dass

Both structures are correct; without `dass` is the journalistic norm.

`Er sagte, **dass** er müde **sei**.` (verb at the end)
`Er sagte, er **sei** müde.` (verb in position 2)

### Tenses in reported speech: the shift

German does **not** shift tenses the way English does. The K-I present can report a present OR a past statement.

`Er sagt: "Ich bin müde."` → `Er sagt, er **sei** müde.`
`Er sagte: "Ich bin müde."` → `Er sagte, er **sei** müde.` (same K-I present)

For a genuinely past event, use the K-I perfect:

`Er sagt: "Ich war müde."` → `Er sagte, er **sei** müde **gewesen**.`

### The Konjunktiv I in the passive

`Der Bericht **werde** geprüft.` (present)
`Der Bericht **sei** geprüft **worden**.` (perfect)
`Die Maßnahme **müsse** ergriffen **werden**.` (modal)

### Reporting verbs and their register

| Verb | Meaning | Register |
|---|---|---|
| sagen | to say | neutral |
| erklären | to state | neutral |
| mitteilen | to inform | formal |
| betonen | to emphasise | formal |
| behaupten | to claim | critical |
| bestreiten | to deny | critical |
| einräumen | to concede | formal |
| angeben | to state | formal/official |
| ausführen | to elaborate | formal |
| darlegen | to set out | formal |

`Der Zeuge **gab an**, er **habe** nichts gesehen.` (official report)
`Der Sprecher **bestritt**, dass es Probleme **gebe**.` (denial)

### Reporting without a verb

Journalistic German often reports with the K-I and no reporting verb at all.

`Die Regierung will die Steuern senken. Der Haushalt **sei** ausgeglichen.` (The government wants to cut taxes. The budget is said to be balanced.)

Here `sei` signals "this is the government's claim, not ours".

### The Konjunktiv I in academic writing

Academic German uses K-I to report sources.

`Müller (2020) **betont**, die Methode **sei** effektiv.` (Müller (2020) stresses that the method is effective.)
`Die Autoren **argumentieren**, das Ergebnis **lasse** sich verallgemeinern.`

### Konjunktiv I vs Konjunktiv II: a summary

| Situation | Form |
|---|---|
| Neutral report, K-I differs from indicative | K-I |
| Report where K-I = indicative | K-II (substitution) |
| Doubtful / distanced report | K-II by choice |
| Wishes, hypotheses, politeness | K-II (different function) |

### Practical C1 rules

1. **Master the third-person singular** (er sage, er sei, er habe, er könne) — the most used form.
2. **Apply the substitution rule** whenever K-I equals the indicative.
3. **Use K-II for doubt**; use K-I for neutral reporting.
4. **Read the stance**: `sei` = neutral, `wäre` = distanced.
5. **Do not shift tenses** the way English does.

**5. Neutral or doubtful?**

a) Der Minister sagte, die Lage ______ unter Kontrolle. (neutral)

a) Der Bericht ______ geprüft. (present)

b) She said she had no time.

d) The minister claimed the situation was under control.

a) Er sagte, er ist müde.
b) Sie sagten, sie haben Zeit.

a) mache
b) sei
c) habe
d) könne
e) werde

**2.**
a) gingen (K-II)
b) hätten (K-II)
c) könnten (K-II)
d) würden (K-II)

**3.**
a) sei
b) habe
c) könne
d) sei ... gewesen

**4.**
a) gingen (K-II)
b) hätten (K-II)

**5.**
a) sei

a) werde
b) sei
c) müsse

a) Er sagte, er sei müde.

## Pronunciation note

`sei` is "zye", `seien` is "ZY-en", `habe` is "HA-be", `könne` is "KÖN-ne". The K-I forms are unstressed; the stress falls on the content word: `Er sagte, er SEI MÜde`.

## Common mistakes

- Wrong: using the indicative in reported speech.
- Wrong: keeping the K-I when it equals the indicative. Right: substitute K-II.
- Wrong: shifting tenses the English way. Right: German keeps the K-I present for present statements.
- Wrong: `dass er sei müde`. Right: `dass er müde sei` (verb at the end) or `er sei müde` (without dass).
- Wrong: using `hätte gewesen` instead of `sei gewesen`.
