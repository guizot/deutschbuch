# German Roadmap: A1 to C2

Reference: Common European Framework of Reference (CEFR), Goethe-Institut level descriptions and Wortlisten.

## What to learn first, and why

German is **front-loaded**. The hard structural work sits at the beginning, unlike English. Three things cause most beginner failure:

1. **Noun gender and article** (`der` / `die` / `das`)
2. **Case** (Kasus: Nominativ, Akkusativ, Dativ, Genitiv)
3. **Word order** (Satzstellung)

So do not start by memorising long vocabulary lists. Start with pronunciation, then gender, then case. Vocabulary grows on top of that skeleton.

### The one habit that matters most

**Never learn a noun without its article.** `Tisch` is wrong. `der Tisch` is right. Also learn the plural: `der Tisch, die Tische`.

Learners who skip this hit a wall at B1, because by then every sentence needs the right case ending and there is no way to guess it. Learners who do it from day one find B1 grammar almost mechanical.

## First 8 topics (A1 opening sequence)

| # | Topic | Why it comes first | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Aussprache (alphabet, sounds) | German is fairly phonetic. Bad reading habits are hard to unlearn. Focus: ä ö ü ß, ch (ich vs ach), sch, ei vs ie, eu/äu, r, z, w, v, st/sp | 2-3 days |
| 2 | Begrüßung und Vorstellung | Immediately usable: Hallo, Guten Tag, Ich heiße..., Ich komme aus... | 2 days |
| 3 | Nomen: Genus, Artikel, Plural | The core foundation. Every noun learned with its article | 1 week |
| 4 | Verben: Präsens, sein, haben | Basic conjugation, including vowel change (ich fahre, du fährst) | 1 week |
| 5 | Zahlen, Uhrzeit, Datum | Used everywhere: prices, schedules, addresses | 3 days |
| 6 | Akkusativ | The first case. It unlocks every other case | 1 week |
| 7 | Modalverben | können, müssen, wollen, möchten, dürfen, sollen | 1 week |
| 8 | Satzstellung | Verb in position 2, questions, separable verbs | 1 week |

## Level map

Vocabulary figures come from the official Goethe Wortlisten (A1, A2, B1). B2 and C1 are linguist estimates, since Goethe stops publishing word lists after B1. Hour figures are the common Goethe and telc ranges.

| Level | Name | Vocabulary | Cumulative hours | Core grammar | Can do |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A1** | Breakthrough | ~650 words | 80-150 | Gender and article, plural, Präsens, sein/haben, Akkusativ, Modalverben, separable verbs, Imperativ, negation, basic word order | Introduce yourself, shop, ask directions, numbers and time |
| **A2** | Waystage | ~1,300 words | 230-350 | Dativ, Perfekt, Präteritum (sein/haben/Modal), reflexive verbs, Nebensätze (weil, dass, wenn, ob), adjective declension, comparative | Daily routines, narrate past events, simple opinions |
| **B1** | Threshold | ~2,400 words | 380-550 | Passiv, Konjunktiv II, Plusquamperfekt, Relativsätze, temporal clauses (als, wenn, nachdem), connectors (deshalb, trotzdem, obwohl), Genitiv | Hold a real conversation, argue, describe experiences, formal letters |
| **B2** | Vantage | ~4,000 words | 530-750 | Passiv with modals, Konjunktiv II past, Nominalisierung, Partizipialkonstruktionen, extended attributes | Debate abstract topics, work or study, presentations |
| **C1** | Effective Operational | ~8,000 words | 750-900+ | Konjunktiv I (reported speech), Passiv-Ersatzformen, Nominalstil, word formation, idiom | Academic and professional register, nuance |
| **C2** | Mastery | 16,000+ | 1,100+ | Stylistics, literature | Near-native |

## What "work or study in Germany" actually requires

| Purpose | Level needed |
|---|---|
| Unskilled or warehouse work | A2 to B1 |
| Most IT and logistics jobs | B1, often strong B1 |
| Skilled trades, nursing, most office jobs | B2 |
| University admission (most programmes) | B2 to C1, plus TestDaF or DSH |
| Medicine, law, teaching, regulated professions | C1 (often C1 Hochschule) |
| Permanent residence and citizenship | B1 (varies by route) |

**Your target is B2.** C1 if you aim for university or a regulated profession. B2 is the level where employers stop asking about language and start asking about your qualifications.

## Certification path

| Level | Exam | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | Goethe-Zertifikat A1, telc Deutsch A1 | Useful as a checkpoint, rarely required |
| A2 | Goethe-Zertifikat A2, telc Deutsch A2 | Checkpoint |
| B1 | Goethe-Zertifikat B1, telc Deutsch B1 | Modular exam. Required for many visas and residence permits |
| B2 | Goethe-Zertifikat B2, telc Deutsch B2 | Modular. The work-ready certificate |
| C1 | Goethe-Zertifikat C1, telc C1 Hochschule, TestDaF | University and regulated professions |
| C2 | Goethe-Zertifikat C2 (GDS) | Near-native proof |

Mock exams are free from Goethe and telc. Take one at the end of each level, not to pass, but to find gaps.

## Weekly rhythm (30 minutes a day, 6 days a week)

| Day | Focus |
|---|---|
| Mon | New lesson (grammar or structure) |
| Tue | Vocabulary + Anki review |
| Wed | Listening (Nicos Weg, Easy German) |
| Thu | New lesson, plus writing 5 sentences using it |
| Fri | Reading aloud, pronunciation |
| Sat | Review the week, speak out loud, Anki |
| Sun | Rest |

At A2 and above, replace one day with a 30-minute conversation session on iTalki or Tandem. Speaking is the skill self-study always neglects, and it is the one exams test hardest.

## Where to step up from 30 to 45-60 minutes

| Point | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| A1 to A2 transition | Habit is stable by now. Extra time goes straight into vocabulary |
| **A2 to B1 transition** | The single biggest jump. Most self-study learners stall here. Extra time pays off most |
| B1 to B2 | Output practice (writing and speaking) needs volume, not just input |
| Before a B2 or C1 exam | Exam technique needs drilling, not just knowledge |
