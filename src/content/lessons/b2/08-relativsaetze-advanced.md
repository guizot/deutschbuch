# Lektion 08: Relative Clauses — Advanced

**Level:** B2 | **Target time:** 30 minutes, repeat over 3 sessions | **Status:** not started

---

## Goal

Use the advanced relative clause types: was/weshalb/weswegen, wer, wo/wohin/woher, and relative clauses with the genitive.

## Why this matters

At B1 you mastered the standard relative pronouns. At B2 you handle the special types that appear in formal writing and that examiners test: `was` clauses, `wer` clauses, and the genitive relatives `dessen`/`deren`. These let you write with precision.

## Material

### Quick review: standard relative pronouns

| Case | M | F | N | Pl |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nom | der | die | das | die |
| Akk | den | die | das | die |
| Dat | dem | der | dem | denen |
| Gen | dessen | deren | dessen | deren |

### Genitive relative pronouns: dessen and deren

`dessen` (m/n) and `deren` (f/pl) mean "whose".

`Der Mann, **dessen** Auto gestohlen wurde, ...` (The man whose car was stolen...)
`Die Frau, **deren** Buch ich lese, ...` (The woman whose book I am reading...)
`Das Kind, **dessen** Eltern berühmt sind, ...` (The child whose parents are famous...)
`Die Leute, **deren** Haus brennt, ...` (The people whose house is burning...)

**Note:** `dessen`/`deren` do **not** take an ending and do **not** change with the case of the thing owned. They agree with the **person** they refer to.

`Der Mann, dessen Auto...` (whose car — the man is masculine → dessen)
`Die Frau, deren Auto...` (whose car — the woman is feminine → deren)

### was as a relative pronoun

Use `was` after:
- `alles`, `nichts`, `etwas`, `vieles`, `manches`
- superlatives (`das Beste`, `das Schönste`)
- a whole preceding clause

`Das ist alles, **was** ich weiß.` (That is all I know.)
`Das ist nichts, **was** mich interessiert.` (That is nothing that interests me.)
`Das ist das Beste, **was** ich je gesehen habe.` (That is the best thing I have ever seen.)
`Er kam zu spät, **was** mich ärgerte.` (He came late, which annoyed me.)

**was with a preposition:** `worauf`, `womit`, `worüber`, etc.

`Das ist alles, **worauf** es ankommt.` (That is all that matters.)

### weshalb / weswegen / wodurch

These relative adverbs refer back to a whole clause and mean "which is why".

`Er kam zu spät, **weshalb** wir ohne ihn anfingen.` (He came late, which is why we started without him.)
`Sie hat nicht gelernt, **weswegen** sie durchfiel.` (She did not study, which is why she failed.)
`Er hat hart gearbeitet, **wodurch** er Erfolg hatte.` (He worked hard, through which he had success.)

### wo / wohin / woher as relative adverbs

Use for places.

`Die Stadt, **wo** ich geboren bin, ...` (The city where I was born...)
`Das Land, **wohin** ich reise, ...` (The country to which I am travelling...)
`Der Ort, **woher** er kommt, ...` (The place from which he comes...)

Or with a preposition: `Die Stadt, **in der** ich geboren bin, ...` Both correct.

### wer as a relative pronoun

`wer` = "the one who", "whoever". It has no antecedent.

| Case | Form |
|---|---|
| Nominativ | wer |
| Akkusativ | wen |
| Dativ | wem |
| Genitiv | wessen |

`**Wer** das sagt, lügt.` (Whoever says that is lying.)
`**Wen** das interessiert, kann kommen.` (Whoever is interested in that can come.)
`**Wem** das gefällt, soll es kaufen.` (Whoever likes it should buy it.)
`**Wessen** Schuld das ist, ist unklar.` (Whose fault that is is unclear.)

**wer clauses often pair with der:**

`**Wer** viel lernt, **der** besteht die Prüfung.` (Whoever studies a lot passes the exam.)
`**Wer** nicht wagt, **der** nicht gewinnt.` (Whoever does not dare, does not win.)

### The correlative der... wer structure

`**Wer** zuletzt lacht, **der** lacht am besten.` (He who laughs last, laughs best.)

This is a fixed, elegant structure. The `der` in the main clause is optional but common in proverbs.

### Relative clauses referring to a whole clause

`Er hat gewonnen, **was** uns alle freut.` (He won, which pleases us all.)
`Sie ist umgezogen, **was** ich nicht wusste.` (She moved, which I did not know.)

### was vs das: the decision

| Antecedent | Pronoun |
|---|---|
| a specific noun | das/der/die |
| alles, nichts, etwas, vieles | was |
| a superlative | was |
| a whole clause | was |

`Das Buch, **das** ich lese, ...` (specific noun → das)
`Alles, **was** ich weiß, ...` (alles → was)
`Das Beste, **was** ich kenne, ...` (superlative → was)
`Er kam spät, **was** mich störte.` (whole clause → was)

### Relative clauses with prepositions (review)

`Preposition + relative pronoun` at the start of the clause.

`Der Mann, **auf den** ich warte, ...`
`Die Firma, **für die** ich arbeite, ...`
`Die Kollegen, **mit denen** ich arbeite, ...`

For things, a `wo-` compound is also possible: `das Buch, **worüber** wir sprechen`.

### Relative clauses in formal writing

Formal German uses relative clauses heavily, often combined with the extended attribute.

`Die Maßnahmen, **die von der Regierung beschlossen wurden**, treten morgen in Kraft.`
= `Die von der Regierung beschlossenen Maßnahmen treten morgen in Kraft.`

The second is denser; the first is clearer. Good writing balances both.

### Practical B2 rules

1. **dessen/deren** for "whose" — agree with the person, not the thing.
2. **was** after alles/nichts/etwas and whole clauses.
3. **weshalb/weswegen/wodurch** to refer to a whole clause.
4. **wer** for "whoever" — no antecedent.
5. **wo/wohin/woher** for places.

## Pronunciation note

`dessen` is "DES-sen", `deren` is "DEH-ren". `weshalb` is "ves-HALP". `wer` is "vair". Keep the relative pronoun light; stress the content word.

## Common mistakes

- Wrong: `Der Mann, deren Auto...`. Right: `dessen` for masculine.
- Wrong: `das` after `alles`. Right: `was`.
- Wrong: using `was` for a specific noun: `Das Buch, was ich lese`. Right: `Das Buch, das ich lese`.
- Wrong: adding an ending to `dessen`/`deren`.
- Wrong: forgetting that `wer` has no antecedent.
