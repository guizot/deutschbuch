# Lektion 04: Konjunktiv I (Indirect Speech)

**Level:** B2 | **Target time:** 30 minutes, repeat over 4 sessions | **Status:** not started

---

## Goal

Report what someone said using the Konjunktiv I, and know when to switch to the Konjunktiv II.

## Why this matters

German journalism, academic writing, and formal reports use the Konjunktiv I for reported speech, to make clear that the words are someone else's and not the writer's claim. It appears in every news article and is tested in reading and writing. This is the single most "B2" structure in German.

---

## Material

### What the Konjunktiv I is

It marks **reported speech**: you are passing on what someone said, without committing to whether it is true.

Direct: `Er sagte: "Ich **bin** müde."` (He said: "I am tired.")
Indirect: `Er sagte, er **sei** müde.` (He said he was tired.)

The Konjunktiv I signals "these are his words, not mine".

### How to form it

From the infinitive stem + the Konjunktiv I endings.

| Person | Ending | machen | sein | haben | werden |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ich | -e | mache | sei | habe | werde |
| du | -est | machest | sei(e)st | habest | werdest |
| er/sie/es | -e | mache | sei | habe | werde |
| wir | -en | machen | seien | haben | werden |
| ihr | -et | machet | seiet | habet | werdet |
| sie/Sie | -en | machen | seien | haben | werden |

**The key form is the third person singular (er/sie/es)**, because that is what reported speech mostly uses: `er mache`, `er sei`, `er habe`, `er werde`.

### The special verb sein

`sein` is irregular and very common in the Konjunktiv I.

| Person | Konjunktiv I |
|---|---|
| ich | sei |
| du | sei(e)st |
| er/sie/es | sei |
| wir | seien |
| ihr | seiet |
| sie/Sie | seien |

`Er sagte, er **sei** krank.` (He said he was ill.)
`Sie sagte, sie **seien** fertig.` (She said they were finished.)

### Modal verbs in the Konjunktiv I

| Modal | Konjunktiv I (er) |
|---|---|
| können | könne |
| müssen | müsse |
| dürfen | dürfe |
| sollen | solle |
| wollen | wolle |
| mögen | möge |

`Er sagte, er **könne** nicht kommen.` (He said he could not come.)
`Sie sagte, wir **sollten** warten.` (She said we should wait.)

### The problem: forms identical to the indicative

For many verbs, the Konjunktiv I and the present indicative look the same in some persons. When they are identical, German switches to the **Konjunktiv II** to keep the "reported speech" signal.

| Verb | Konjunktiv I | Indicative | Use instead |
|---|---|---|---|
| kommen (er) | komme | kommt | komme (differs, fine) |
| gehen (wir) | gehen | gehen | **gingen** (K-II) |
| haben (wir) | haben | haben | **hätten** (K-II) |
| sein (wir) | seien | sind | seien (differs, fine) |

`Sie sagten, sie **gingen** nach Berlin.` (They said they were going to Berlin — K-II because K-I would be identical.)
`Sie sagten, sie **hätten** Zeit.` (They said they had time — K-II.)

**Rule:** if the Konjunktiv I form equals the indicative form, use the Konjunktiv II instead.

### The reporting verbs

| Verb | Meaning |
|---|---|
| sagen | to say |
| erklären | to explain, state |
| behaupten | to claim |
| berichten | to report |
| mitteilen | to inform |
| betonen | to emphasise |
| zugeben | to admit |
| bestreiten | to deny |
| erwähnen | to mention |
| äußern | to express |

`Der Minister **erklärte**, die Lage **sei** unter Kontrolle.` (The minister stated that the situation was under control.)
`Der Sprecher **betonte**, es **gebe** keine Probleme.` (The spokesman stressed that there were no problems.)

### No dass needed

With the Konjunktiv I, you can drop `dass` and keep normal word order.

`Er sagte, **dass** er müde **sei**.` (with dass, verb at the end)
`Er sagte, er **sei** müde.` (without dass, verb in position 2)

Both are correct. Without `dass` is more common in journalism.

### Tenses in the Konjunktiv I

The Konjunktiv I has its own present, past, and future forms.

| Time | Konjunktiv I | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Present | K-I present | Er sei müde. |
| Past | K-I perfect | Er sei müde gewesen. |
| Future | K-I future | Er werde kommen. |

`Er sagte, er **sei** krank.` (present)
`Er sagte, er **sei** krank **gewesen**.` (past)
`Er sagte, er **werde** kommen.` (future)

### Konjunktiv I in the passive

`Der Bericht **werde** geprüft.` (The report is said to be checked.)
`Das Gesetz **sei** geändert **worden**.` (The law is said to have been changed.)

### Where you meet the Konjunktiv I

| Context | Example |
|---|---|
| News articles | Der Präsident sagte, er sei zufrieden. |
| Academic writing | Die Studie zeigt, die Methode sei effektiv. |
| Formal reports | Der Zeuge gab an, er habe nichts gesehen. |
| Literature | Er dachte, sie sei gegangen. |

### Konjunktiv I vs Konjunktiv II in reported speech

| Situation | Form |
|---|---|
| K-I differs from indicative | Use K-I |
| K-I equals indicative | Use K-II |
| Uncertain/doubtful statement | Often K-II |

The Konjunktiv II adds a note of doubt or distance.

`Er sagt, er **sei** krank.` (neutral report)
`Er sagt, er **wäre** krank.` (doubtful — "supposedly")

### Practical B2 rules

1. **In reported speech**, use the Konjunktiv I for the third person.
2. **If the K-I form equals the indicative**, switch to K-II.
3. **`sein` is irregular**: sei, seien.
4. **You may drop `dass`** and keep normal word order.
5. **In your own writing**, use K-I to report sources; do not overuse it in casual text.

## Pronunciation note

`sei` is "zye", `seien` is "ZY-en". The Konjunktiv I forms are unstressed in the clause; the stress falls on the content word: `Er sagte, er SEI MÜde`.

## Common mistakes

- Wrong: using the indicative in reported speech: `Er sagte, er ist müde`. Right: `er sei müde`.
- Wrong: keeping the indicative when the K-I form is identical. Right: switch to K-II (`sie gingen`, `sie hätten`).
- Wrong: `dass er sei müde` (mixing dass with position 2). With `dass`: `dass er müde sei`. Without: `er sei müde`.
- Wrong: forgetting that `sein` is irregular (sei, seien).
- Wrong: overusing the Konjunktiv I in casual conversation; it belongs to formal/reporting registers.
