# Lektion 15: Adjektivdeklination (Full Review)

**Level:** B1 | **Target time:** 30 minutes, repeat over 5 sessions | **Status:** not started

---

## Goal

Put the correct ending on any adjective before a noun, in any case, with any article type.

## Why this matters

Adjective endings are the most feared part of German grammar, but they follow one simple logic: **the ending carries the information that the article does not**. Once you see it that way, it stops being random. It is heavily tested and appears in every written text.

---

## Material

### The core principle

German wants the gender and case of the noun to be **visible**. The article shows it if it can. If the article is weak or absent, the **adjective** shows it instead.

`**der** gute Wein` (the article shows masculine nominative)
`**ein** gut**er** Wein` (the article does not show it, so the adjective does)

This is the whole system. Everything else follows.

### The three declension types

| Type | Trigger | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Weak** | after der/die/das, dieser, jener, jeder, alle | der gute Wein |
| **Mixed** | after ein/eine, kein, possessive (mein, dein...) | ein guter Wein |
| **Strong** | no article at all | guter Wein |

### Type 1: Weak declension (after der-words)

After `der`, `die`, `das`, `dieser`, `jener`, `jeder`, `mancher`, `welcher`, `solcher`, `alle`, the adjective takes **-e** or **-en**.

| | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter | Plural |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nominativ | -e | -e | -e | -en |
| Akkusativ | -en | -e | -e | -en |
| Dativ | -en | -en | -en | -en |
| Genitiv | -en | -en | -en | -en |

**Examples:**
`der gut**e** Wein` (nom. m)
`den gut**en** Wein` (akk. m)
`dem gut**en** Wein` (dat. m)
`die gut**e** Suppe` (nom./akk. f)
`der gut**en** Suppe` (dat./gen. f)
`das gut**e** Bier` (nom./akk. n)
`die gut**en** Weine` (plural, all cases -en)

**Shortcut:** weak declension is mostly **-e** in the nominative singular (all genders) and **-en** everywhere else.

### Type 2: Mixed declension (after ein-words)

After `ein`, `eine`, `kein`, `mein`, `dein`, `sein`, `ihr`, `unser`, `euer`, `Ihr`, the adjective takes the **strong** ending where the article has no ending, and **-en** elsewhere.

| | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter | Plural |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nominativ | -er | -e | -es | -en |
| Akkusativ | -en | -e | -es | -en |
| Dativ | -en | -en | -en | -en |
| Genitiv | -en | -en | -en | -en |

**Examples:**
`ein gut**er** Wein` (nom. m — article has no ending, adjective takes -er)
`einen gut**en** Wein` (akk. m)
`einem gut**en** Wein` (dat. m)
`eine gut**e** Suppe` (nom./akk. f)
`einer gut**en** Suppe` (dat. f)
`ein gut**es** Bier` (nom./akk. n — article has no ending, adjective takes -es)
`keine gut**en** Weine` (plural)

**Shortcut:** in the nominative, the adjective takes the ending that the article is missing: `ein guter`, `eine gute`, `ein gutes`.

### Type 3: Strong declension (no article)

With no article, the adjective takes the **definite article's ending**.

| | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter | Plural |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nominativ | -er | -e | -es | -e |
| Akkusativ | -en | -e | -es | -e |
| Dativ | -em | -er | -em | -en |
| Genitiv | -en | -er | -en | -er |

**Examples:**
`gut**er** Wein` (nom. m)
`gut**en** Wein` (akk. m)
`gut**em** Wein` (dat. m)
`gut**e** Suppe` (nom./akk. f)
`gut**er** Suppe` (dat./gen. f)
`gut**es** Bier` (nom./akk. n)
`gut**en** Weinen` (dat. plural)

### The master table (all three types, all cases)

| Case | M | F | N | Pl |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Weak** (der) | | | | |
| Nom | -e | -e | -e | -en |
| Akk | -en | -e | -e | -en |
| Dat | -en | -en | -en | -en |
| Gen | -en | -en | -en | -en |
| **Mixed** (ein) | | | | |
| Nom | -er | -e | -es | -en |
| Akk | -en | -e | -es | -en |
| Dat | -en | -en | -en | -en |
| Gen | -en | -en | -en | -en |
| **Strong** (none) | | | | |
| Nom | -er | -e | -es | -e |
| Akk | -en | -e | -es | -e |
| Dat | -em | -er | -em | -en |
| Gen | -en | -er | -en | -er |

### The 5 rules that cover 90% of cases

1. **Dative and genitive are almost always -en.** (Except strong dative m/n = -em, f = -er.)
2. **Plural is always -en** except strong nominative/accusative (-e).
3. **Feminine and neuter nominative/accusative are the same** (-e weak, -e/-es mixed and strong).
4. **Masculine nominative** shows the ending: -e (weak), -er (mixed/strong).
5. **If the article shows the case, the adjective is weak (-e/-en).**

### Adjectives after nichts, etwas, viel, wenig

These are treated as strong (no article).

`etwas Neu**es**` (something new)
`nichts Gut**es**` (nothing good)
`viel Interessant**es**` (a lot of interesting things)
`wenig Neu**es**` (little new)

### Adjectives with two or more

Each adjective takes its own ending.

`ein gut**er**, alt**er** Wein` (a good, old wine)
`der gut**e**, alt**e** Wein`
`gut**er**, alt**er** Wein`

### Participle as adjective

Participles follow the same rules.

`ein interessierend**es** Buch` (an interesting book)
`das geschrieben**e** Buch` (the written book)

### Adjective endings in comparative/superlative

The comparative and superlative take the same endings.

`ein schnell**erer** Wagen` (a faster car)
`der schnell**ste** Wagen` (the fastest car)
`ein gut**es** Ergebnis` → `ein bess**eres** Ergebnis`

## Pronunciation note

Adjective endings are light and unstressed: `gute` is "GOO-te", `guten` is "GOO-ten", `gutes` is "GOO-tes". Do not over-pronounce the ending; it is a quick, soft syllable.

## Common mistakes

- Wrong: `ein gute Wein`. Right: `ein guter Wein`. Mixed masculine nominative → -er.
- Wrong: `der guten Wein` (nominative). Right: `der gute Wein`. Weak nominative → -e.
- Wrong: `ein gutes Wein`. Right: `ein gutes Bier` — but for Wein: `ein guter Wein`. Check the noun's gender.
- Wrong: forgetting -n in the dative plural: `mit den gute Weinen`. Right: `mit den guten Weinen`.
- Wrong: treating `etwas` as needing an article: `etwas neue`. Right: `etwas Neues`.
