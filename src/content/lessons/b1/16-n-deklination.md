# Lektion 16: Die n-Deklination (Weak Nouns)

**Level:** B1 | **Target time:** 30 minutes, repeat over 3 sessions | **Status:** not started

---

## Goal

Recognise and use the weak masculine nouns that add -en/-n outside the nominative singular.

## Why this matters

A small but important group of masculine nouns behaves differently: they add **-en** or **-n** in the accusative, dative, and genitive singular. They are common (der Student, der Junge, der Mensch, der Name) and getting them wrong is a visible error in exams and formal writing.

---

## Material

### What the n-Deklination is

Weak masculine nouns add **-en** or **-n** in all cases **except the nominative singular**.

| Case | der Student | der Junge |
|---|---|---|
| Nominativ | der Student | der Junge |
| Akkusativ | den Student**en** | den Junge**n** |
| Dativ | dem Student**en** | dem Junge**n** |
| Genitiv | des Student**en** | des Junge**n** |

**Only the nominative singular is normal.** Everywhere else, add -en or -n.

### The two sub-groups

| Ending | Rule | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **-en** | most weak nouns | Student, Mensch, Nachbar, Kollege, Präsident, Polizist |
| **-n** | nouns ending in -e | Junge, Name (+ns), Kunde, Neffe, Franzose, Russe |
| **-ns** | Name, Gedanke, Wille, Buchstabe | des Name**ns**, des Gedanke**ns** |

### Recognising weak nouns: the patterns

Most weak nouns fall into these groups:

1. **Masculine nouns ending in -e**: der Junge, der Kunde, der Neffe, der Kollege, der Löwe.
2. **Masculine nouns ending in -ent, -ant, -ist, -at, -et**: der Student, der Präsident, der Demonstrant, der Journalist, der Soldat, der Architekt.
3. **Masculine nouns ending in -ph, -loge**: der Philosoph, der Biologe.
4. **Nationalities in -e**: der Franzose, der Russe, der Türke.
5. **A few irregular ones**: der Mensch, der Nachbar, der Herr, der Bauer, der Held, der Bär.

### Adjectives with weak nouns

The adjective follows the normal rules; only the noun changes.

`ein gut**er** Student` (nominative)
`einen gut**en** Student**en**` (accusative — both adjective and noun take -en)
`einem gut**en** Student**en**` (dative)
`des gut**en** Student**en**` (genitive)

### Weak nouns in the plural

Weak nouns take **-en/-n** in the plural too (they do not add -s).

| Singular | Plural |
|---|---|
| der Student | die Student**en** |
| der Junge | die Junge**n** |
| der Mensch | die Mensch**en** |
| der Kollege | die Kollege**n** |
| der Name | die Name**n** |

**So the plural and the non-nominative singular look the same.** Context (article, verb agreement) tells you which.

`Der Student kommt.` (singular)
`Die Studenten kommen.` (plural)
`Ich sehe den Studenten.` (singular accusative)
`Ich sehe die Studenten.` (plural accusative)

### Weak nouns vs strong nouns

| Strong (normal) | Weak (n-Deklination) |
|---|---|
| der Lehrer → des Lehrers | der Student → des Studenten |
| der Mann → des Mannes | der Mensch → des Menschen |
| der Freund → des Freundes | der Nachbar → des Nachbarn |

Strong nouns take -s/-es in the genitive. Weak nouns take -en/-n in the genitive, accusative, and dative.

### Practise with a sentence

`Ich sehe **den Studenten**.` (accusative — weak noun takes -en)
`Ich helfe **dem Studenten**.` (dative — -en)
`Das ist das Buch **des Studenten**.` (genitive — -en)
`**Der Student** ist hier.` (nominative — no ending)

### Feminine and neuter nouns: no n-Deklination

The n-Deklination only affects **masculine** nouns. Feminine and neuter nouns behave normally.

`die Studentin → der Studentin` (feminine, no change)
`das Kind → des Kindes` (neuter, normal -es)

Note: `der Student` (m) is weak, `die Studentin` (f) is normal.

## Pronunciation note

`Student` is "shtu-DENT", `Studenten` is "shtu-DEN-ten". The -en ending is a light syllable. `Name` is "NAH-me", `Namens` is "NAH-mens".

## Common mistakes

- Wrong: `Ich sehe den Student`. Right: `den Studenten`.
- Wrong: `dem Kollege`. Right: `dem Kollegen`.
- Wrong: `des Name`. Right: `des Namens`.
- Wrong: applying n-Deklination to strong nouns: `des Lehreren`. Right: `des Lehrers`.
- Wrong: assuming feminine nouns are weak: `der Studentin`. Right: `der Studentin` (feminine, no change).
