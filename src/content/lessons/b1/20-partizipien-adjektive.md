# Lektion 20: Partizipien als Adjektive

**Level:** B1 | **Target time:** 30 minutes, repeat over 3 sessions | **Status:** not started

---

## Goal

Use Partizip I and Partizip II as adjectives before nouns, and understand the difference in meaning.

## Why this matters

German turns verbs into adjectives freely: `das lachende Kind` (the laughing child), `das geschriebene Buch` (the written book). This is compact and common in writing and news. It also lets you replace relative clauses elegantly, which examiners reward.

---

## Material

### Two participles

| Participle | Form | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Partizip I** | infinitive + d | active, ongoing |
| **Partizip II** | ge...t / ge...en | passive, completed |

### Partizip I: active and simultaneous

`Partizip I` = infinitive + **d**. It means the noun is **doing** the action, right now.

| Verb | Partizip I | Example |
|---|---|---|
| lachen | lachend | das lachend**e** Kind (the laughing child) |
| singen | singend | der singend**e** Mann (the singing man) |
| laufen | laufend | die laufend**e** Frau (the running woman) |
| schlafen | schlafend | das schlafend**e** Baby (the sleeping baby) |
| weinen | weinend | das weinend**e** Mädchen (the crying girl) |

`Das **lachende** Kind spielt im Garten.` (The laughing child plays in the garden.)

The child is laughing — active.

### Partizip II: passive and completed

`Partizip II` means the noun has **received** the action, or the action is **finished**.

| Verb | Partizip II | Example |
|---|---|---|
| schreiben | geschrieben | das geschrieben**e** Buch (the written book) |
| bauen | gebaut | das gebaut**e** Haus (the built house) |
| kochen | gekocht | das gekocht**e** Essen (the cooked food) |
| brechen | gebrochen | das gebrochen**e** Fenster (the broken window) |
| öffnen | geöffnet | die geöffnet**e** Tür (the opened door) |

`Das **geschriebene** Buch ist berühmt.` (The written book is famous.)

The book has been written — passive/completed.

### The key contrast

| Partizip I | Partizip II |
|---|---|
| active (the noun does it) | passive (the noun receives it) |
| ongoing | completed |
| das lachende Kind | das geliebte Kind |
| (the child who is laughing) | (the child who is loved) |

### Adjective endings apply

Participles as adjectives take normal adjective endings.

`ein lachend**es** Kind` (neuter nominative, mixed → -es)
`der lachend**e** Mann` (masculine nominative, weak → -e)
`die geschrieben**e** Arbeit` (feminine nominative → -e)
`das gebaut**e** Haus` (neuter nominative → -e)

### Partizip I with zu: gerundive (muss/soll)

`zu + Partizip I` means "to be done" (obligation or possibility).

`die **zu lösende** Aufgabe` (the task to be solved)
`das **zu bezahlende** Formular` (the form to be paid)
`die **zu lesenden** Bücher` (the books to be read)

**Note:** with `zu + Partizip I`, the `zu` goes before the verb: `zu lösende`, `zu bezahlende`.

This is formal and common in writing.

### Replacing relative clauses

Participle constructions replace relative clauses and sound more formal.

| Relative clause | Participle construction |
|---|---|
| das Kind, das lacht | das lachende Kind |
| das Buch, das geschrieben wurde | das geschriebene Buch |
| die Aufgabe, die gelöst werden muss | die zu lösende Aufgabe |
| der Mann, der singt | der singende Mann |

This transformation is a classic B1/B2 exam task.

### Extended participle phrases (recognition)

German often builds long participle phrases before the noun.

`die **von Goethe geschriebenen** Werke` (the works written by Goethe)
`der **in Berlin lebende** Künstler` (the artist living in Berlin)
`das **gestern gekaufte** Auto` (the car bought yesterday)

The structure: article + [extra information] + Partizip + adjective ending + noun.

These are common in formal writing and news. At B1, recognise them; producing them is a B2 skill.

### Partizip I vs Partizip II: choosing

| Question | Use |
|---|---|
| Is the noun doing the action? | Partizip I |
| Is the noun receiving the action, or is it finished? | Partizip II |

`das **interessierende** Thema` (the topic that interests — active)
`das **interessierte** Publikum` (the interested audience — passive)

### Common Partizip I adjectives (now fixed adjectives)

Some Partizip I forms have become normal adjectives.

| Form | Meaning |
|---|---|

| interessant (from interessierend) | interesting |
| dringend | urgent |
| folgend | following |
| passend | suitable |

## Pronunciation note

The participle carries the stress on its stem: `LAchende`, `geSCHRIEbene`. In `zu lösende`, the stress is on `LÖSende`.

## Common mistakes

- Wrong: `das lachen Kind`. Right: `das lachende Kind` (Partizip I adds -d).
- Wrong: `das schreiben Buch`. Right: `das geschriebene Buch` (Partizip II).
- Wrong: `die zu lösend Aufgabe`. Right: `die zu lösende Aufgabe` (adjective ending).
- Wrong: confusing the active Partizip I with the passive Partizip II.
- Wrong: forgetting the adjective ending after the participle.
